## Saturation lookup and sea-ice oracles

Both fixtures below were produced by the harnesses in
`tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle`, calling the unmodified WRF v4.6.1
`phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F` at commit
`d66e442fccc04111067e29274c9f9eaccc3cef28`, compiled with
`GNU Fortran (Ubuntu 13.3.0-6ubuntu2~24.04.1) 13.3.0` at `-O0`.  Every row
carries its complete input state, so each row is reproducible on its own.
Unlike the snow fixtures, these two are reproduced by gpuwm today:
`gpuwm.core.ruc.ruc_qsn` and `gpuwm.core.ruc.ruc_sea_ice_step` match both
files bit-for-bit, and `validate_qsn_oracle.py` / `validate_sice_oracle.py`
assert exactly that inside `build.sh`.

`oracle/sice.csv` drives `sice`, the snow-free sea-ice column: heat
diffusion through the nine ice levels plus the surface energy balance closed
by `vilka`.  The ice column properties `tice`, `rhosice`, `capice` and
`thdifice` are built from the ice temperature profile with the same Zubov
relations `lsmruc` uses and are written to the file; the surface humidity is
seeded through `qsn`, as `lsmruc` seeds it.  Its eight columns are deep cold
ice, ice at the melting point where the `min(271.4, ...)` cap binds on the
skin, thin ice with its base pinned at the ocean freezing point, a strongly
forced balance that drives the RUC evaporation branch, moist air that drives
the RUC condensation branch, the same condensing state with `myj=.true.`,
a `myj=.true.` evaporating state, and rain falling on near-melting ice.

exactly as in `snowseaice`.  `sice` itself never writes the soil water

`smfrkeep=1`, `keepfr=0` in an identical loop after both call sites

the call so the pinned composite matches what `ruc_sea_ice_step` returns.

## Snow oracles

`gpuwm/core/ruc.py` and `gpuwm/core/ruc_gpu.py` port all four snow branches
`sfctmp` dispatches to: the shared snow-preparation prologue
(`ruc_snow_preparation`), `snowtemp` (`ruc_snow_temperature_step`),
`snowsoil` (`ruc_snow_soil_step`) and `snowseaice`
(`ruc_snow_sea_ice_step`), each with a CUDA twin.  Every fixture in this
section is compared against on both lanes: the `validate_*_oracle.py`
scripts `build.sh` runs fail closed on any difference, and
`tests/test_ruc.py` / `tests/test_ruc_gpu.py` assert the same parity
bitwise.

Each of the four original fixtures is paired with a second,
argument-contract fixture, because a bitwise match against a file whose
arguments are flat pins the arithmetic and not the argument list.  Mutation
studies over the finished ports measured how weak the originals were alone:
13 of 14 argument-drop mutants reproduced `snowseaice.csv`, 72 of 344
argument read sites were undetectable by `snowtemp.csv`, and five arguments
(`qcatm`, `snom`, `sat`, the entry `qsg`, the entry `ilnb`) were undetectable
by `snowsoil.csv`.  The `sfctmp` prologue has no pre-existing fixture and no
callable interface at all; it is pinned by reading the running, unmodified
module with gdb.

Except where a block below says otherwise, every fixture in this section was
produced by the harnesses in `tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle`, calling the
unmodified WRF v4.6.1 `phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F` at commit
`d66e442fccc04111067e29274c9f9eaccc3cef28`, compiled with
`GNU Fortran (Ubuntu 13.3.0-6ubuntu2~24.04.1) 13.3.0` at `-O0`.  Every row
carries its complete input state, so each row is reproducible on its own.
The nine-level soil grid, the 5001-entry `tbq` table and a 60 s step are the
same ones the snow-free fixtures use.

One caveat applies to the snow-covered *land* lanes only.  `soilprop`,
`transf`, `soilmoist` and the freezing curve reach these fixtures bitwise,
and the CPU and CUDA lanes reach each other bitwise, only because those
routines now round a float64 transcendental once instead of calling numpy's
or the device's float32 `exp`/`pow`/`log10`.  That substitution is
PROVISIONAL and is NOT glibc -- see `_RUC_PROVISIONAL_TRANSCENDENTALS` in
`gpuwm/core/ruc.py` for the measured deviation from the live glibc 2.39.
`gpuwm/core/noahmp_libm.py` and `r_log`/`r_log10`/`r_exp`/`r_pow` in
`gpuwm/core/kernels/noahmp_leaves.cu` already carry verified glibc 2.39
transcriptions of exactly these four calls; the RUC lanes should be moved
onto them and this paragraph deleted once those are promoted out of the
Noah-MP namespace.  No fixture in this file discriminates between the two
implementations, which is why the discrimination lives in `ruc.py`'s note
and not here.

`oracle/snowtemp.csv` drives `snowtemp`, the snow energy budget and the
snow/soil heat-diffusion solve.  Its six columns are one-layer shallow fresh
snow (`rhosn` 100, 0.20 m), a two-layer aged pack (`rhosn` 350, 0.43 m), a
ripe two-layer pack that melts from the top (top melt, `rsm` retention and

harness with the same arithmetic `snowsoil` performs, and are all written to
the file.  `snowtemp` declares `ilnb` `intent(out)` but reads it on the
blended path, so `ilnb_before` is pinned as part of the input state.

It exists because bitwise parity is only as strong as the fixture's ability
to notice the port being wrong.  A mutation study over the finished port
(`tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle/mutation_study_snowtemp.py`: replace one read of
one argument at a time, then ask whether the fixture still reproduces) found
that `snowtemp.csv` alone cannot detect 72 of 344 argument read sites being
wrong, and leaves six reachable `if` arms unexecuted.  `tranf`, for one, is
read only in the evaporation half of the melt energy budget
(`phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F:5436-5443`) and could be zeroed with no effect on
any output column.  Its eight regimes are: melt under evaporation on a dense
pack; melt where the remaining pack can evaporate entirely, so `beta` is
rewritten inside the melt block (`:5483-5491`); melt on blended snow capped
by the available snow (`:5510-5512`); unlimited two-layer bottom melt with
`hsn = snhei-deltsn` (`:5638`); bottom melt limited by `rr = snwe/delt`
(`:5663`); low-density new snow on an aged pack, which selects the `:5049`
conductivity branch through its second disjunct; unlimited melt under
condensation; and an above-283 K skin that releases both Egglston guards
(`:5499` and `:5658`), which is the only way to combine an unlimited melt
with a Koren density update.  Produced by the same harness directory, the
same pinned `phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F` at
`d66e442fccc04111067e29274c9f9eaccc3cef28`, and the same
`GNU Fortran (Ubuntu 13.3.0-6ubuntu2~24.04.1) 13.3.0` at `-O0` as every
fixture above, via `build_snowtemp_contract.sh` rather than `build.sh` so it
regenerates without rebuilding any already-pinned CSV.

With both files the study leaves 31 arithmetic survivors, every one of them
a WRF dead store (`:5076`, `:5159-5160`, `:5164-5165`, `:5172`,
`:5180-5181`, `:5191-5194`, `:5207`), unreachable on the supported domain
(`h` is fixed at 1 at `:5209` so the `:5315-5328` retry cannot be entered,
and `snhei == 0` is rejected because `LSMRUC` gates the snow branch on
`snhei > 0` at `:1600` and `:3580` is the module's only `call snowtemp`,

`oracle/sfctmp_prep.csv` pins the `sfctmp` snow-preparation block,

straight-line code at the top of `sfctmp`, and every result is a local or an

`isice=15`) for the first fourteen cases and USGS-RUC (`URBAN=1`,
`isice=24`) for the last three.

`oracle/sfctmp.csv` pins WRF `sfctmp` end to end -- the snow-preparation
prologue AND the dispatch plus mosaic recombination at
`phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F:1767-2196`.  The prologue needed gdb because its
results are locals; the dispatch does not, because it is the tail of the
subroutine and the returned arguments ARE its outputs.  `run_sfctmp.F90`
therefore just supplies forcing and calls the unmodified `sfctmp`, recording
every `intent(inout)`/`intent(out)` argument on both sides of the call: 160
columns, 33 regimes x 9 levels.  Cases 1-17 are the preparation harness's
forcing spliced in verbatim, so both fixtures drive `sfctmp` from
byte-identical state and any residue here localises to the dispatch.

The 33 regimes bind every arm: mosaic land (7 columns) and mosaic sea ice (3)
so each recombination has two independent snow fractions and cannot be
reproduced by returning an endpoint; non-mosaic snow on land (14) and on sea
ice (7); snow-free land (7) and snow-free sea ice (2), split across the
`if(alb.ne.albice)` rescale at `:2158` and the leg that skips it; the `:2077`
melt-out reset on the mosaic path, the non-mosaic path and -- case 33 -- on
sea ice; the `:2111` urban cap; both legs of the `:1997-2001` `keepfr`
selection; and the only column that drives `runoff2` off zero.  Case 29 is
the only column in the EVAPORATION regime, which is what makes `pc` and `cn`
readable at all (`transf` consults `pc` only when `lai <= 4`, `:6415-6422`,
and `(cst/sat)**cn` only matters under the 0.25 cap at `:2597`); case 32 is a
second such column on a land-use row with a different `pc`.

`validate_sfctmp_oracle.py` replays every column through
`gpuwm.core.ruc.ruc_surface_temperature_step` and then hard-codes each of the
routine's arguments to each distinct value it takes, requiring every such
mutant to fail: 741 mutants, five survivors, each argued unreachable in that
file with the WRF line that kills it (`soilice`, `soiliqw`, `qcg` and `s` are
rewritten or forced before any read on all six paths; `ilnb` is the

## The driver

`oracle/lsmruc.csv` pins WRF `LSMRUC` end to end,
`phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F:84-1175` -- the RUC land-surface driver that composes
everything above.  `LSMRUC` is a subroutine whose results are all returned
arguments, so unlike the `sfctmp` prologue it needs no debugger:
`run_lsmruc.F90` supplies forcing and calls the unmodified routine, recording
the complete `intent(inout)`/`intent(out)` argument set on both sides plus the
complete forcing.  122 numeric columns, 48 (run, step, column) groups x 9
levels.

Two independent runs, each integrated for two steps, because several branches
are selected by call scalars rather than by column state:

* run 1 -- `MODIFIED_IGBP_MODIS_NOAH` (MODI-RUC), `frpcpn=.true.`,
  `rdlai2d=.false.`, `dt=60`, `iswater=17`, `isice=15`
* run 2 -- `USGS` (USGS-RUC), `frpcpn=.false.`, `rdlai2d=.true.`, `dt=180`,
  `iswater=16`, `isice=24`
* step 1 is `ktau=1`, the first-step initialisation block (`:481-565`); step 2
  is `ktau=2` on the state step 1 left behind, so `sfcrunoff`, `acrunoff`,
  `sfcevp`, `snowfallac`, `acsnow` and `snom` are pinned with a nonzero
  incoming value rather than from zero.  **`udrunoff` is not** -- this line
  used to claim it was, and the corrected mutation study below measured
  otherwise; see the fixture gaps.

The twelve columns per run bind: both `frpcpn` arms and, inside the
`.not. frpcpn` arm, both sides of the 273.15 K split; water; sea ice above the
threshold and land with `0 < xice < threshold`; both `iforest` arms, so both
`meltfactor` values and both root-depth searches; all three `ktau==1` `soilt1`
arms and both the `qcg` and `qvg` reset arms; a column that drives `chklowq` to
0; snow-free columns that take the `:1082` `lemitbl` emissivity; a sea-ice
column whose `snowfrac` is scaled by `xice` at `:1105`; thin, deep, melting and
freshly-fallen snow; and urban.  `tests/test_ruc.py::test_lsmruc_binds_every_driver_arm`
asserts every one of those is non-empty.

### The mutation study, and the vacuous control it used to have

`validate_lsmruc_oracle.py` replays every group through
`gpuwm.core.ruc.ruc_land_surface_step` and then hard-codes each driver argument
to each distinct value the fixture gives it.  It used to report **218 mutants,
zero survivors**.  That number was meaningless.

The study scored a mutant as *killed* when its output was not bitwise-equal to
`lsmruc.csv`.  The unmutated port is not bitwise-equal to `lsmruc.csv` either
-- that is the 26-cell `tanhf` residue documented below -- so the **null
mutant, the port with nothing changed, was itself "killed"**, and every mutant
scored as detected whether or not the fixture could see it.  Re-run against the
correct control, the port's own output, the same 218 mutants leave **30
survivors**.  The single assertion that would have caught this, "the unmutated
port must survive", now runs first, in the validator (`_null_mutant`) and in
`tests/test_ruc.py::test_lsmruc_mutation_control_is_the_port_not_the_fixture`.

A surviving constant has two possible causes and only one of them is
unreachability: either the port never reads the argument, or it does and the
surviving constant is simply the only value the fixture ever lets that read
see.  A second study separates them and is the negative control for every
claim below -- it replays the whole fixture with one argument taken from
*another* case, leaving all others alone, at shifts 1 (crosses the column-type
boundary inside a call), 12 (crosses the timestep) and 24 (crosses the run).

**Unreachable: 6 arguments, 22 of the 30 survivors.**  No supported call can
read the entry value, and no permutation of them moves any output.

| `chs` | read at `:681`-`:682` only, both inside `if(myj)`; `ruc_land_surface_step` rejects `myj=True`, as `ruc_soil_step` and `ruc_snow_soil_step` already do |

Both tables are exact in both directions: a survivor in neither fails the
validator, a listed entry that stops surviving fails it, an "unreachable"
argument that any permutation kills fails it, and a "fixture gap" that no
permutation kills fails it.  `tests/test_ruc.py` carries the same three
measurements.

**No port defect.**  Every one of the 30 survivors is a property of the
fixture, not of `ruc_land_surface_step`: at each of the eight live reads the
port reads the argument exactly where WRF does, including the two easy ones to
get wrong -- the water arm's `continue` leaving `lh`, `qfx`, `hfx`, `sfcexc`,
`z0`, `grdflx` and the runoff accumulators as pass-throughs, and
`ruc_surface_parameters` preserving the caller's `znt` at the water category.

**What the driver does not copy, and what it reproduces.**  WRF accumulates
`sfcevp` twice per land column, at `:1095` and again at `:1116`, with nothing
in between changing `qfx`; the fixture shows `sfcevp` advancing by
`2*qfx*dt`.  That is a WRF defect, and since development branch WOOF
accumulates it once; the fixture comparisons grade `sfcevp` against WRF's
entry value plus `qfx*dt` once.  `rhosnf`, `precipfr` and `snowfallac` are declared `intent(out)` at
`:344-347` but `rhosnf` is read at `:695` and `snowfallac` is read inside
`SFCTMP` at `:1641`, so both are live inputs in practice and the port takes
them as such.  `:1109-1141` (`wb`, `waterbudget`, `acwaterbudget`) and
`:906-908` are dead and are not transcribed.

**ARW additions after the historical driver fixture.**  The `EM_CORE==1`
precipitation partition (`:618-652`) and lake bypass are transcribed in the
forecast path and independently checked by the surface-forcing source probe.
The bypass follows WRF's `:824` test, `lakemodel==1 .and. lakemask==1`: with
`sf_lake_physics = 0` a `LAKEMASK = 1` column runs the water branch, and only
`sf_lake_physics = 1` hands it to the CLM lake model.
The old whole-driver fixture remains an explicit `em_core=0` replay because
that is how it was compiled. `spp_lsm = 1` is transcribed
(`gpuwm/core/ruc_spp.py`, graded against `spp_oracle/` by
`tests/test_ruc_spp.py`): the forecast path takes a member-owned
`pattern_spp_lsm`/`field_sf` pair from the ensemble provider, and an enabled
consumer with no pattern is refused. `mosaic_lu` and `mosaic_soil` accept 0 or 1:
`ruc_surface_parameters` and `gpuwm/core/ruc_mosaic.py` transcribe the
weighted `LANDUSEF`/`SOILCTOP` parameters and the irrigation block at
`:984-1009`, graded against `oracle/mosaic_surface.csv` and
`oracle/mosaic_driver.csv` (`tests/test_ruc_mosaic.py`). `myj=.true.` is
rejected because `ruc_soil_step` and `ruc_snow_soil_step` are, which makes
`:681-682` unreachable; that arm is transcribed but carries a comment saying
it is unverified.

**Uninitialised reads.**  `SFCTMP`'s `ilnb` (`:1385`) carries the previous
COLUMN's snow-layer count inside the driver loop; `ruc_land_surface_step`
threads that chain explicitly and returns the final value.  The driver's own
`snoh`, `snflx`, `s`, `sublim` and `evapl` automatics are read by `SFCTMP` and
are only zeroed at `ktau==1`, so from `ktau>1` WRF reads stack residue; gpuwm
defines them as zero on every call.  `oracle/lsmruc_stackfill.csv` is the same
driver with the callee stack filled with `12345.0` instead of zeroed and is the
negative control for both: over 432 rows x 122 numeric columns the pair differs
only in `tsnav` (36 cells) and in the `tsnav` carried into the next step's
entry snapshot (18 cells), on the two thin-snow columns.  So on this fixture
`ilnb` is observable and the five stale locals are not.  The validator and
`tests/test_ruc.py` both assert that difference set exactly.

**Residue, and a defect it found in `ruc_snow_preparation`.**  26 of the
fixture's cells do not reproduce bitwise.  None of them is the driver's own
arithmetic: every one is a straight pass-through of an
`ruc_surface_temperature_step` output.  25 of the 26 are a single function.
`gpuwm.core.ruc._f32_tanh` transcribes **fdlibm**'s `tanhf`, and glibc 2.39's
`tanhf` is not fdlibm's: rebuilding the fdlibm form from glibc's own `expm1f`
gives `0.760541856` where glibc `tanhf` returns `0.760541916`, at the argument
`x = 0.9974991083145142` that WRF `:1520` reaches with `tabs = 270 K`.  Over
the 80 `tanh` arguments this fixture reaches, `_f32_tanh` misses glibc on four,
by up to 3 ULP.  Substituting a measured glibc `tanhf` table for `_f32_tanh`
and replaying collapses the residue from 26 cells / 425 ULP to **one cell of
1 ULP** -- `grdflx` on `(run 1, step 2, mixed_forest_melt)`, which is the
`exp`/`pow`/`log10` class `_RUC_PROVISIONAL_TRANSCENDENTALS` already documents.

Measured over the arguments WRF `:1520-1521` reaches for air temperatures from
220 K to 310 K in 0.01 K steps -- 18,000 distinct float32 arguments, both the
`0.15` and the `0.3333` forms -- `_f32_tanh` misses glibc 2.39 `tanhf` on 231
of them, 1.28%, by up to 3 ULP.  So this is not a single unlucky argument: any
RUC fixture that lands on one of those 1.28% will show it.

That is a defect in `ruc_snow_preparation`, not in the driver.  It is pinned
cell by cell rather than fixed here because fixing it needs a verified glibc
`tanhf` transcription, its CUDA twin in `gpuwm/core/kernels/ruc.cu`, and a
re-verification of `oracle/sfctmp_prep.csv` and `oracle/sfctmp.csv`.
`oracle/lsmruc.csv` is the first RUC fixture to reach an argument where the two
`tanh` implementations disagree, which is why the snow lanes were bitwise
without it.  A listed cell may only shrink; any unlisted nonzero cell fails, in
both the validator and the test.

## Soil ingest -- `oracle/soil_ingest.csv`

This one is not `phys/module_sf_ruclsm.F` at all.  It is `real.exe`'s side of
RUC: `share/module_soil_pre.F`'s `init_soil_depth_3` (:1153-1194) and
`init_soil_3_real` (:1817-2158), the pair `process_soil_real` (:817-830)
selects when `sf_surface_physics == RUCLSMSCHEME`.  It exists because RUC was
admitted as a scheme with **no way to initialise it from real data**, and a
prior lane correctly declined to invent a remap with no oracle behind it.

The build in `tools/ruc_soil_ingest_wrf461_oracle` runs WRF's own `.F -> .f90`
pipeline (`configure.wrf:365-370`, including `tools/standard.exe`, which is
what rewrites `CALL wrf_error_fatal (` into `wrf_error_fatal3 ( __FILE__ ,
__LINE__ ,`) and then **checks its output against the `.f90` WRF's own build
left in `share/`** -- they are byte-identical, which is the receipt that the
preprocessing is WRF's and not a hand-patch.  `frame/module_state_description.F`
is compiled from the pinned tree rather than stubbed, because
`process_soil_real` branches on the scheme integers it defines.  Only seven
symbols are stubbed, exactly the ones `nm -u` reports on a full build's
`share/module_soil_pre.o`.

and without `flag_sm_adj` and with and without an SST field, a layer source at
the GFS/Noah depths, a node source already on RUC's nine levels with the
adjustment both off and armed (where it must be a no-op), and one arm handing
the layers in deepest-first.

* **2 rows, the all-zero-moisture land column under `flag_sm_adj`.**  :2079

* The Noah source bucket at :1930-1934 weights the four source layers

  `init_soil_depth_2`); the 2 m total agrees, the middle two do not.

## Operational-branch oracle (`oracle_fork/`)

`oracle_fork/` holds the WRF v4.6.1 harnesses above
(`tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle/run_*.F90`, unchanged) compiled against the
operational RAP/HRRR branch's `module_sf_ruclsm.F` (NOAA-EMC/HRRR v4.1.21,
`sorc/hrrr_wrfarw.fd/WRFV3.9/phys`) instead of WRF v4.6.1's, with that
branch's `run/` tables.  The inputs are the v4.6.1 oracle's, so each case runs
the same column through the other lineage.  `tests/test_ruc_fork_oracle.py`
grades the port's `wrf_45` names (`ruc_soilprop`, `ruc_snow`, `ruc_irrigation`
and `ruc_qvg_cold_start = "air"`) against it: SOILPROP, the snow-free soil
step, SNOWTEMP and SNOWSOIL bit for bit. SFCTMP and LSMRUC retain their
documented, pinned residues; their SNOWFALLAC increments are compared after
conversion from the branch's metres to the port's millimetres. The fork
device decks in `tests/test_ruc_fork_gpu.py` also hold SNOWTEMP and SNOWSOIL
to every oracle output word. Routines the two lineages share give the same numbers under
both builds and are not repeated.  Rebuild with


## Prescribed monthly surface fields

`oracle/monthly_interp.json` contains 525 float32 outputs for 35 columns
on 15 dates, including the year wrap and leap day. The generator
`tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle/build_monthly_interp.py` compiles the unchanged
`monthly_interp_to_date` routine at lines 7545-7611 of the v4.1.21 fork.
The source SHA256 is
`e45234bd25dbc7a5d747384cb78781da1feda92f97bdcf6f691f3807c3b006d2`.
Calendar conversion and the interior-point predicate are the only stubs.
GNU Fortran 15.2.0, `-O0`, generated the fixture.

The prescribed LAI and monthly background albedo paths round each REAL
multiply, sum and division to float32. The historical float64 seed remains
when `rdlai2d` is false. `tests/test_ruc_veg_albedo_seaice.py` compares every
output bit with this fixture and verifies that using the float64 seed is a
detectable negative control.

## The driver under the operational HRRR surface switches

`oracle/lsmruc_hrrr_switches.csv` is the same unmodified `LSMRUC`
(`module_sf_ruclsm.F` sha256
`3265f810d08dcbddfaf198371dc7f652e78e8d3a788f703a515c555a3bbb2a12`) driven by
`tools/ruc_wrf461_oracle/run_lsmruc_frac.F90`, built by `build_lsmruc_frac.sh`:
`run_lsmruc.F90` with `xice_threshold = 0.02` (the value the surface driver
passes under `fractional_seaice = 1`) and `rdlai2d = .true.` in both runs, the
sea-ice columns at xice 0.25, 0.04 (run 1) and 0.45, 0.12 (run 2), inside the
band the switch opens, and two land columns at 0.01 and 0.015, below it.

The HRRR v4.1.21 fork's `LSMRUC` (`sorc/hrrr_wrfarw.fd/WRFV3.9/phys/
module_sf_ruclsm.F`, sha256
`cb2b54114afc247e16ea03d547a510b19842e2ee012da1202eba19105bef6aab`) reads
`xice_threshold` in the same two expressions as this module (fork :845 and
:1127, here :860 and :1106) and `rdlai2d` only to skip the table LAI in
`SOILVEGIN` (fork :7028, :7042, :7061, :7075; here :6867, :6880, :6899,
:6913). This confirms the decision expressions and the 4.6.1 wiring proof.
It does not make the whole 4.6.1 column a substitute for the older fork.

A direct v4.1.21 build with the same driver and the fork's own tables was
also replayed with `soilprop="wrf_45"`. It differed in 866 returned words;
the LAI field remained bitwise identical. A stack-fill control changed
four TSNAV column outputs. These are whole-column differences beyond the
monthly-field and threshold wiring. The result is not a column parity
qualification for the older fork.

Toolchain: GNU Fortran 13.4.0 and 15.2.0 give byte-identical CSVs (sha256
`6eef7b59a6eb0f8fe0dc94a20d3a4fefb5ff96cfbcf519c8e02e349d28b7229f`) on glibc
2.43, not the 13.3.0 / glibc 2.39 build that pinned `lsmruc.csv`.  The libm
change moves the residue map: the same build run on `run_lsmruc.F90` differs
from the pinned `lsmruc.csv` in 455 words (snow density, snow fraction, ground
flux and the surface fluxes of the snow columns), and the port's own replay of
that rebuilt fixture leaves 31 words, max 26 ULP, in the same classes.  The
switch fixture leaves 30 words, max 26 ULP, in the same classes; on the eight
sea-ice groups the switch moves, every word is WRF's bit for bit except one
1-ULP `qsg` word that the rebuilt 0.5-threshold fixture also leaves on the same
column.  `tests/test_ruc_lsmruc_hrrr_switches.py` pins the map and runs the two
negative controls (the 0.5 pin and the table LAI each break the fixture).

Rebuilt on the test host (development branch) with `build_lsmruc_frac.sh` on the
13.3.0 / glibc 2.39 toolchain `lsmruc.csv` was pinned on (sha256
`8f9fea614ebc5b253773d149cc7dcfaf3aaaf8b260917bdbb3d3d60c030bc170`).  With
RUC on WOOF's float32 libm words the port reproduces it bit for bit except
SFCEVP, which WRF counts twice and WOOF once, so the pinned map is empty.
`oracle/mosaic_surface.csv` and `oracle/mosaic_driver.csv` were rebuilt the
same way (`tools/ruc_mosaic_wrf461_oracle/build.sh`; pins in
`gpuwm.core.ruc_contract`): their gfortran 15.2 build carried that C
library's words (grdflx 1-2 ULP) and the 2.39 build is matched bit for bit.
The `oracle_fork/` CSVs are left as built; rebuilt on W1 their sfctmp case
reproduces with no residue and the LSMRUC one differs only in `chklowq` and
in run-2 `znt`/`z0` that the current harness seeds differently, so replacing
them is a fixture regeneration for the fork lineage, not done here
(`tests/test_ruc_fork_oracle.py` pins their measured maps).
