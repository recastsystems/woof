# Growth referees

The removed failure was a referee error, not evidence for changing a source coefficient. A 240-hour run was required to satisfy 0.24 <= g Hs/U10² <= 0.27 at both winds. The primary ST4 paper explicitly says full development does not exist because the spectrum continues growing slowly (2010 JPO40 p1925, DOI 10.1175/2010JPO4324.1). Its actual duration comparisons are spectra after 8 and 48 hours at 10 m/s (Figures4–5). Equations19–24 use wave-age-dependent wind input and a wind-dependent, wave-supported stress closure. Replacing u* with a fixed multiple of U10 is not that closure.

No source coefficient, stress conversion, tail cutoff, dissipation floor, or transport coefficient changes here. The independent 2016 swell update already on the branch is retained. Its source and limits remain in SCIENCE.md.

Use `python -m woof.ocean.waves.validation.published_growth OUTPUT` for one bounded default-ST4 duration/fetch experiment at 10 and20 m/s. The receipt records source hashes, spectra, u*, fixed600s step, physical clock, and finite fetch profiles. `--analyse` redraws saved results without integrating. Charts compare separate empirical referees; no coefficient is selected by them. The wrapper reports DONE, not an automatic scientific PASS.

The finite paper estimates in published_spectra.json were read from vector paths, not fitted to this solver. For the 2010 Figure5 TEST441 curve, the ordinate is0.15 E(f); integrating the plotted spectrum gives approximately Hs2.255m, fp0.12811Hz. The present 48h10m/s values are2.12580m and0.13700Hz (−5.72% and+6.94%). The 8h peak is0.19906Hz versus0.20695Hz. These peak differences are less than one frequency-bin ratio (1.1). Do not infer an8h wave height from the normalized source-term panel without confirming its spectral ordinate scale.

A second primary comparison, DOI10.1175/JPO-D-18-0137.1 Figure4(e), supplies20m/s ST4D spectra after2,4,8,16,24,32,48h. It uses beta_max1.33, a50-bin grid of ratio1.07, and is not identical to the2010 TEST441 configuration (beta_max1.52, here36 bins of ratio1.1). At8–48h our Hs differs by+0.63,+0.52,−0.91,−1.47,−2.03%; fp differs by+4.66,+1.87,−1.86,−0.52,+4.77%. The initial2h state differs more (Hs−17.35%, fp+14.81%); seed and configuration differences have not been isolated by another integration. This is supporting finite-growth evidence, not bitwise reproduction of that study.

Fetch references are JONSWAP1973 and the1992 composite/stability-dependent curves. DOI10.1175/JPO-D-18-0137.1 Eq16 gives the u*-scaled stable curve epsilon*=.0021 chi*^.79 and nu*=2.3/(2pi) chi*^−.25, with3e4<=chi*<=6e6. Outside that field range it is an extrapolation and must not be scored as measured truth. At the saved20m/s fetch center, X382.034km, u*=.99694m/s, chi*=3.7695e6, epsilon*=297.85 versus329.22 (−9.53%), nu*=.009164 versus.008308 (+10.30%). The saved10m/s center lies outside the range (chi*=2.360e7) and is not scored. Older receipts do not contain u* at every fetch cell; their U10-scaled profiles cannot be relabelled u*-scaled profiles.

The same2019 paper Eq17 explicitly converts U10-scaled PM values through a specified wind-dependent Cd. Its ST4D mature estimate is epsilon*~1000, nu*~.0058; that is not a universal U10-normalized height. The2004 wave-interaction text, DOI10.1017/CBO9780511525018 p61 Eqs2.121–122, explains how wind-dependent drag gives a stronger wind-speed dependence in Hs than U10² for a u*-scaled state. This explains the direction of the old wind-speed discrepancy, not the exact magnitude of every late state.

At240h, present Hs is2.52544/14.02567m at10/20m/s (U10-normalized height .24766/.34386). Both still grow. These are finite-time measurements. A PM convergence claim or a universal fully-developed ST4 limit would be unsupported. The solver is qualified here for the finite published growth comparisons and the separate whole real-case skill test, with this long-duration limit stated openly.
